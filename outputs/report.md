## Host-Nation Advantage at the FIFA World Cup

### Summary
- Host nations earn on average **0.55 more points per game** (2.00 vs 1.45) and improve their **goal difference by 0.72 goals per game** at home compared to the same teams' other World Cups. This advantage is statistically significant (p < 0.001).
- The home advantage in points per game is present in all eras (1930-1950, 1954-1994, 1998-2022), though it shows a slight, statistically insignificant downward trend over time (p = 0.12). The goal difference advantage has declined significantly over time (p = 0.034).
- Hosts have a much higher chance (59.1%) of reaching the last four when hosting, compared to 23.0% in their other tournaments, a 36.1 percentage point improvement.

---

## Data and Method

This study analyzed every FIFA World Cup host nation's performance in their home tournament versus their performance in all other World Cups they participated in. The advantage was measured by:

- **Points per game (ppg):** Calculated with modern scoring (3 points for a win, 1 for a draw), counting shootouts as draws.
- **Goal difference per game (gdpg):** Difference between goals scored and conceded per match.

West Germany and Germany were combined as one team. Qatar 2022 was excluded since it has no non-host World Cup appearances for comparison.

To test for significance, permutation tests were employed. Each host team's own World Cups were randomly relabeled as "home" or "other" to create a null distribution controlling for team strength but assuming independence between teams.

Teams' likelihood of reaching semi-finals (last four) at home versus elsewhere was also compared.

---

## Results

### Overall Home Advantage

| Metric         | At Home | Other World Cups | Advantage | Significant?       |
|----------------|---------|------------------|-----------|--------------------|
| Points per Game | 2.00    | 1.45             | +0.55     | Yes (p < 0.001)    |
| Goal Difference per Game | 0.83 | 0.11            | +0.72     | Yes (p < 0.001)    |

Out of 22 hosts analyzed, 18 improved their points per game and goal difference when hosting.

### Notable Hosts

- **Largest home advantage in points per game:**
  - Uruguay 1930: 3.00 at home vs 1.38 elsewhere (+1.62)
  - England 1966: 2.67 vs 1.50 (+1.17)
  - Chile 1962: 2.00 vs 1.04 (+0.96)

- **Hosts that performed worse at home:**
  - France 1938: 1.50 at home vs 1.70 elsewhere (-0.20)
  - Spain 1982: 1.00 vs 1.69 (-0.69)
  - Brazil 2014: 1.57 vs 2.21 (-0.64)

- **Host nations that won the World Cup at home:**
  - Uruguay (1930), Italy (1934), England (1966), Germany (1974), Argentina (1978), France (1998).

### Chances of Reaching Semi-Finals

| Situation           | Probability of Last Four |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Hosting             | 59.1% (13 out of 22)    |
| Other World Cups     | 23.0%                   |
| **Improvement**     | +36.1 percentage points |

Being the host significantly raises the chance of reaching the semi-finals.

## Has the Advantage Changed Over Time?

The home advantage by eras is summarized below:

| Era        | Home ppg | Other ppg | ppg Gap | P-value | Home gdpg | Other gdpg | gdpg Gap | P-value |
|------------|----------|-----------|---------|---------|-----------|------------|----------|---------|
| 1930-1950  | 2.32     | 1.76      | 0.56    | 0.031   | 1.87      | 0.56       | 1.31     | 0.003   |
| 1954-1994  | 1.99     | 1.36      | 0.63    | <0.001  | 0.67      | -0.05      | 0.72     | <0.001  |
| 1998-2022  | 1.83     | 1.42      | 0.41    | 0.012   | 0.50      | 0.11       | 0.39     | 0.067   |

Trends over decades:

- **Points per game gap:** decreases by 0.058 per decade (p = 0.12, not significant)
- **Goal difference gap:** decreases by 0.142 per decade (p = 0.034, significant)

**Interpretation:**

The goal difference advantage has significantly diminished over time, while the points per game advantage shows a smaller, statistically non-significant decline. The home advantage remains substantial in all eras despite this weakening trend.

---

## Limitations

- Sample sizes differ by era (4 hosts in 1930-1950, 11 in 1954-1994, 7 in 1998-2022), which affects the power and reliability of era comparisons.
- Tournament formats and the number of matches varied over time, potentially impacting comparability of PPG and GDPG.
- Confounding factors such as host nation investments before tournaments, automatic qualification, home crowd support, referee bias, and travel demands (e.g., from Pollard 2008) are not controlled here.
- The permutation test assumes independence across host teams and treats outcomes within teams as exchangeable, which may not capture all complexities.
- Qatar 2022 was excluded since it has no other World Cups, limiting analysis of very recent effects.

---

## Conclusion

Host nations do perform significantly better at their home World Cups compared to their usual World Cup results, confirming the existence of a measurable “host-nation advantage.” This advantage amounts to roughly half a point more per game and a substantially better goal difference, along with markedly improved chances of reaching the semi-finals.

The evidence supports the hypothesis that this advantage has partially diminished over time, shown notably in goal difference but not definitively in points per game. The home advantage remains substantial, though some recent hosts (e.g., Brazil 2014, Spain 1982) performed worse at home than in their other World Cups.

Overall, hosting the FIFA World Cup conveys a significant but evolving competitive edge.
